A tankless water heater does not store hot water at all. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is efficient and space-saving, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In Racine, WI, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

Error codes are a starting point, not a verdict, and we treat them that way on every Racine call. The same ignition failure code can come from a worn igniter, a fouled flame rod, a gas line too small to feed the burner at full fire, or an air intake clogged with lint. That is why Racine Water Heater tests before replacing anything. Our technicians read the fault history stored in the control board, watch flow sensor output with a faucet open, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, and put a manometer on the gas inlet to see what pressure actually reaches the valve when the burner is running flat out. We also compare inlet and outlet temperatures against the flow rate, which exposes a scaled heat exchanger long before it fails outright.

We work on tankless heaters from Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Takagi, and the other major brands, gas-fired and electric alike. The fix depends on the diagnosis: a full descaling flush through the isolation valves when scale is choking the heat exchanger, a new flow sensor when the unit no longer notices a faucet opening, a fresh igniter or cleaned flame rod when ignition keeps failing, or a vent correction when the unit trips on combustion air. Sometimes the answer is bigger. A gas line undersized for the unit's full firing rate has to be upsized, and a heat exchanger cracked by years of scale may make replacement the smarter call on an older unit. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • Repeated ignition attempts, then a shutdown
  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower
  • The display showing an error code instead of a temperature
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time
  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse
  • New whistling, roaring, or rumbling from the unit

The Benefits of Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair

Consider the risks first. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. Electric tankless models are serious electrical loads, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

A trained technician with proper test equipment gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. We descale with a circulating pump and the right solution, then flush thoroughly so nothing corrosive is left in your plumbing. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, we will say so before you spend money on a repair.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Do not keep resetting a recurring code; note it and have it checked
  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • If scale comes back quickly, add a softener or a scale-prevention cartridge on the cold inlet
  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
