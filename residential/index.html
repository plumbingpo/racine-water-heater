No Surprises

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Racine Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing for every home, covering everything from a burned-out heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod slowly dissolves, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no visible warning. If you notice rusty hot water, popping or rumbling sounds, a rotten-egg smell, shorter hot showers than you're used to, or moisture around the base, schedule an inspection right away. An early diagnosis usually keeps a fixable issue from becoming a replacement.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually tells us. Once a tank passes the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one decides for you. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Preventive Care

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service heads off failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Racine Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific equipment, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a look at the burner and flue on gas models, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

Most homes do best with a yearly flush, and houses on hard water often need a visit every six months. We adjust the schedule to the age of your heater, how hard your household runs it, and local water quality, which determines how quickly sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

The savings show up faster than most people expect. A flushed tank transfers heat with less effort, a replaced anode rod keeps corrosion off the tank walls, and a tested relief valve means the tank can safely vent pressure if something goes wrong. Compared with the cost of replacing a tank early, regular maintenance is money well spent.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

Getting the size right matters more than most homeowners realize. Before recommending a new heater, Racine Water Heater works through the details that drive your household's peak demand: the number of people and bathrooms, back-to-back showers, when the laundry and dishwasher run, and your incoming water temperature. That gives you a unit with a first-hour rating or GPM capacity to cover your busiest hour without wasting energy on excess capacity.

Picking a new water heater comes down to more than the number of gallons. We'll compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, laying out operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, while a well-sized tank is often the simpler, lower-cost choice. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

Homes in Racine run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Racine Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that never gets hot enough, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, rumbling tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The correct fix depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it tends to be a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes are solved with an expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before the damage spreads. We also check the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. A tank leaking from the steel itself can't be patched, and we'll tell you so. Any gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility right away.
