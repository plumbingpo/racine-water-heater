Keeping Your Mount Pleasant Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one with water on the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.

Common Water Heater Problems in Mount Pleasant

In Mount Pleasant, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tanks usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units lose elements and thermostats, especially when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
